Lesson 1 · Paychecks+

Gross pay vs. net pay

Why $480 earned isn't $480 in the bank.

Gross pay is your wage times your hours. Net pay (take-home pay) is what's left after deductions.

  • FICA takes 7.65% of almost every paycheck: 6.2% for Social Security and 1.45% for Medicare.
  • Federal income tax is often $0 for teens with part-time jobs, because income under the standard deduction isn't taxed. Your employer may still withhold some.
  • State income tax depends on where you live. Some states have none.
Try this: If tax was withheld but you earned under the standard deduction, filing a tax return can get that money refunded.
Lesson 2 · Budgeting+

The 50/30/20 rule

A simple way to split every dollar you earn.

A budget is a plan for your money before you spend it. The 50/30/20 rule is an easy starting point:

  • 50% needs: gas, phone bill, car insurance, school costs.
  • 30% wants: food with friends, games, clothes, concerts.
  • 20% savings: emergency fund, college, a car, investing.

If you live at home with few bills, flip it: save more now while your costs are low.

Lesson 3 · Investing+

Compound interest

How money earns money, and why time matters most.

Compound interest means you earn returns on your original money and on the returns it already earned. The longer you wait, the faster it grows.

  • Rule of 72: divide 72 by your yearly return to estimate how many years it takes to double. At 8%, money doubles about every 9 years.
  • A Roth IRA lets anyone with earned income invest for retirement, and qualified withdrawals later are tax-free. If you're under 18, a parent opens a custodial one with you.
  • Stock market returns are never guaranteed. Prices go up and down, especially in the short run.
Try this: In the growth calculator, compare starting at 16 vs. starting at 26.
Lesson 4 · Credit+

Credit scores and debt

The three-digit number that follows you for years.

A FICO credit score runs from 300 to 850. Lenders, landlords, and even some employers check it. Here's roughly what goes into it:

  • 35% payment history: do you pay on time?
  • 30% amounts owed: how much of your credit limit you use.
  • 15% length of history, 10% new credit, 10% credit mix.

Credit cards often charge over 20% APR. Carry a balance and that interest compounds against you.

Try this: Pay your full statement balance every month and you'll never pay credit card interest.
